# Autonomous Warehouse Forklifts

**Product Research & Development Intern · Tensor Group · Summer 2026**

I conducted the initial research for an autonomous forklift that could pick up and move pallets in warehouses. I examined how existing autonomous forklifts are built, what safety standards apply to this class of equipment, and what it could cost to build, deploy, and service one. I consolidated the findings into English and Persian reports and prepared a model catalogue and engineering references for the development team.

## About Tensor Group

Tensor Group is a Tehran-based robotics and industrial automation company. Its logistics work includes mobile parcel-sorting robots deployed at Iran's National Post Company, according to the company's public profile. [Tensor Group on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/tensor-team/)

## What I worked on

| Area | My contribution | Where to review it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product benchmarking | Compared autonomous pallet transporters, stackers, and reach trucks by payload, lift height, aisle requirements, navigation, charging, and integration features. | Report, Part I, Chapters 3–7, and the catalogue's `Sheet1` and `Update Log` tabs |
| Hardware and autonomy | Studied vehicle structure, drive and steering, mast and fork actuation, onboard computing, and sensors such as LiDAR, depth cameras, and encoders. I synthesized published examples into a reference architecture covering navigation, machine control, and safety systems. | Report, Part II-A, Chapters 13–31 |
| Safety standards | Reviewed published requirements for driverless industrial trucks, including ISO 3691-4, and documented functional safety, testing, and site commissioning considerations. | Report, Part II-B, Chapters 32–43 |
| Market, buyers, and costs | Researched Iranian and international suppliers, potential buyers, adoption trends, equipment sales and service models, and cost drivers for development, deployment, and ongoing support. I discussed how local labor costs and warehouse workflows could affect the business case in Iran. | Report, Part I, Chapters 4 and 8–12 |
